Builds

01

Light-Seeking Turret

Two axis turret that finds the brightest point in the room and locks onto it. It calibrates on startup, sweeps to scan, compares the light readings to work out which way to move, then confirms the lock with a laser and a buzzer.

Engineering
Analogue sensing, differential comparison, servo control, startup calibration
Hardware
Arduino Uno R3, SG90 servos, LDRs, laser module, buzzer

02

Light-Following Robot

Two wheeled robot that steers toward a light source using a pair of LDRs, one on each side. The difference between the two readings sets the steering, and the speed scales with how far off it is. A deadband stops it hunting when the light is straight ahead.

Engineering
Differential drive, proportional steering, deadband handling, motor driver control
Hardware
Arduino, paired LDRs, DC motors, motor driver, 3D printed chassis

03

Temperature Control System

Thermostat built around an NTC thermistor and a relay driven heater. The Beta equation converts resistance to temperature, and hysteresis around the setpoint stops the relay chattering on and off at the threshold. The relay is driven to a safe off state before the pin is configured, so it cannot flick on during boot.

Engineering
Thermistor characterisation, Beta equation, hysteresis control, safe startup state
Hardware
Arduino, NTC thermistor, relay module, heating element

How I work

I test each subsystem on its own before connecting anything to anything else. Sensors get checked against something I already know the answer to, motors get bench tested off the ground, and only then does the loop get closed.

Every project folder holds the sketch, the wiring, the bench tests I ran, and the limitations I have not solved yet. The bring up sketches are in there alongside the final code, because they are usually where the real debugging happened.
